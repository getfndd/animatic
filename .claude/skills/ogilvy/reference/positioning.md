# Positioning Framework

How a product is placed against alternatives, and the questions positioning has to answer before any copy is written. Load for `@ogilvy position`, or when the headline problem is really a positioning problem.

---

Every product, feature, or page needs a positioning statement. Use this structure:

```
For [target user] who [situation/pain],
[Product] is a [category] that [key benefit].
Unlike [alternatives], we [differentiator].
So that [outcome].
```

### Rules for Good Positioning

1. **Target user** must be specific. "Startups" is too broad. "Seed-to-Series B founders running their first fundraise" is specific.
2. **Situation/pain** must be real and felt. Not theoretical. Interview-derived, not assumed.
3. **Category** must be recognizable. Users need a mental shelf to put you on.
4. **Key benefit** must be singular. One claim, provable.
5. **Differentiator** must be honest. If competitors also do it, it is not a differentiator.
6. **Outcome** must matter to the buyer. Not to your team.

### Positioning Depth Levels

| Level | Scope | Example |
|-------|-------|---------|
| **Product** | The whole product | "For seed-stage founders who fundraise for the first time..." |
| **Module** | A product area | "For founders managing their cap table..." |
| **Feature** | A specific capability | "For founders sharing a data room with investors..." |
| **Moment** | A specific interaction | "When an investor requests additional documents..." |

Go deeper as the context narrows.
