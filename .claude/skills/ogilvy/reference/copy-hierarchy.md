# Copy Hierarchy

How a page orders its claims — what leads, what supports, what closes. Load when structuring a landing page or judging whether copy is in the right order.

---

Every marketing surface follows the same information architecture:

### 1. Headline
- One claim, provable, specific
- 6-12 words ideal, never more than 15
- Must pass the Swap Test
- Must work without reading anything else on the page

### 2. Subhead
- Expands the mechanism — how does the headline happen?
- 15-25 words
- Can introduce secondary benefit
- Lower energy than headline — informational, not persuasive

### 3. Body Copy
- Proof points, specifics, social proof
- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
- Bullet points for scanners
- Every paragraph must earn its space — if it does not add proof or clarity, cut it

### 4. Call to Action
- Specific action, low commitment
- Verb + object: "Start your data room", "See your cap table"
- Never "Submit", "Sign up", "Get started" (too generic)
- The CTA should tell users what happens next, not just what they click

### Hierarchy Rules

| Rule | Rationale |
|------|-----------|
| Never make the same claim twice | Repetition signals weak copy |
| Each level adds new information | Don't restate — expand |
| Body proves what headline claims | If it doesn't prove, cut it |
| CTA matches headline promise | "Track investors" CTA after "pipeline" headline |
